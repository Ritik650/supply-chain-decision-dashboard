# Decision memo

_For: head of operations. Data: DataCo Smart Supply Chain, Jan 2015 – Sep 2017. Generated by `memo/build_memo.py` from `outputs/findings/`; the PDF is the version to send._

**Cap discounts at 10%: an estimated $240k–$371k more profit a year (central $371k), because discounts above 10% sell no extra units. Prove it in one market for four weeks before rolling it out.**

## 1. Cap discounts at 10% in every category (Dashboard page 3)

- **Action.** Set a 10% maximum discount for all categories.
- **Evidence.** Items discounted above 10% sell 2.18 units per order line vs 2.18 at or below (within-product elasticity -0.006, 95% CI -0.057 to +0.044), and their margin is 11.8% vs 12.1% (p = 0.29). 73,106 items (44%) carried $1.02M of discount above 10% in 33 months.
- **Impact.** $240k (low) / $371k (central) / $371k (high) profit a year. It still pays unless more than 42% of the capped items' volume is lost.
- **Test first.** Apply the cap in one market for 4 weeks against a holdout market; compare orders per customer and conversion, not just units per line.

## 2. Change the First Class and Second Class promises (Dashboard page 2)

- **Action.** Promise First Class in 2 days. Either make Second Class faster than Standard or promise it in 4 days.
- **Evidence.** First Class is late on 100% of items: it always takes 2 days against a 1-day promise. Second Class is 79.8% late and takes 3.99 days, no faster than Standard (3.99, p = 0.85). Shipping mode explains lateness (Cramér's V 0.48); region does not (0.02).
- **Impact.** Late rate falls from 57.3% to 41.9% (First Class only) or 34.1% (both), with no operational change. No revenue gain is claimed: late first orders reorder at the same rate (32.8% vs 32.8% within 90 days).
- **Test first.** Show the new promise at checkout in one market for 4 weeks; track First and Second Class take-up, conversion and complaints.

## 3. Don't fund RFM-targeted win-back; test one broad retention offer (Dashboard page 4)

- **Action.** Run any retention offer as a randomised holdout across all customers, not on an RFM list.
- **Evidence.** Scored on data to March 2017, Champions ordered again in the next six months at 54.2% vs 55.5% for Hibernating (all six segments 54%–57%, p = 0.45; AUC 0.49–0.50). Month-3 retention is a flat 12.7% with no decay.
- **Impact.** Each +1 pt of monthly repeat rate (now 12.5%) is worth about $100k profit ($823k revenue) a year. A 0.5–2 pt lift, if the test finds one, would be $50k–$200k; that lift range is illustrative, not measured.
- **Test first.** Hold out 20% of customers at random for 8 weeks; scale only if the lift covers the offer's cost.

## Assumptions and limits

- **Observational data.** Every result is an association. Discounts were spread evenly across rates (9,096–9,182 items per rate), which rules out targeting bias but not period effects.
- **Orders that never happened are invisible.** The data only holds completed orders, so it cannot show buyers who would not order without a discount. That is why recommendation 1 is a test first. The cap assumes fixed unit cost; the dataset's own profit ratio does not move with discount, which a real cost structure would.
- **Scope.** Values treated as USD (the data dictionary gives no currency; no conversion to rupees). Cancelled and suspected-fraud items (7,754) excluded. Window January 2015 – September 2017 (33 months): from October 2017 the source switches to single-item orders and a new catalogue. Annual figures = window total × 12/33.
- **Products are not the lever.** Profit is as concentrated as revenue (top 20% of products: 96.1% of profit, 96.1% of revenue) and only 2 small products lose money, so no delisting is recommended (page 5).

## Next steps

- Discount cap: the 4-week market holdout, plus site-visit data (the access logs) to measure conversion, not just basket size.
- Delivery: carrier hand-off timestamps to see why Second Class ships no faster than Standard; complaint and return data to price lateness.
- Retention: the randomised holdout, and the cost per contacted customer to set the break-even lift.

## Appendix: method

Method. Cap recovery = Σ over items with applied rate r > 10% of (r − 0.10) × gross sales, × 12/33 (high, volume holds). Central multiplies each capped item's new profit by exp(β × (0.10 − r)) with the measured β floored at 0; low by (1 − 1.5 × price rise). Break-even loss = recovery ÷ (current profit of capped items + recovery). Sources: notebooks/q1–q4, sql/analysis/p2_repromise_whatif.sql, p3_whatif_cap.sql, p4_rfm_validation.sql.
