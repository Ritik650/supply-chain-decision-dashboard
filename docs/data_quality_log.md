## 1. Day-1 checks

- **Row count.** 180,519 order items, 65,752 orders, 20,652 customers, 118 products. Order dates 2015-01-01 to 2018-01-31.
- **Encoding.** The CSV is latin-1, not UTF-8 (city names such as `Ciudad de Almatý`). DuckDB's own `latin-1` reader rejects the file, so the build transcodes it to UTF-8 (`data/interim/`) before loading. Line endings are CRLF.
- **Masked or empty columns.** `Customer Email` and `Customer Password` hold one value (`XXXXXXXXX`); `Product Description` is 100% empty; `Product Status` is constant 0; `Order Zipcode` is 86% null. All dropped.
- **Duplicate measures.** Six column pairs are byte-identical on every row (rule T07), e.g. `Benefit per order` = `Order Profit Per Order` and `Sales per customer` = `Order Item Total`. One of each pair is dropped. `Order Profit Per Order` is item-level despite its name: `profit_ratio x item_total` reproduces it.
- **Discount rate.** Every row listed at 6% was discounted 5.5% (rule T10). The build uses the applied rate (`discount / sales`) and keeps the listed one as `discount_rate_listed`.
- **Negative profit.** 32,295 valid order items (18.7%) lose money, totalling -3,708,393. They stay in every KPI: they are real losses, not errors.
- **Order status.** COMPLETE 59,491, PENDING_PAYMENT 39,832, PROCESSING 21,902, PENDING 20,227, CLOSED 19,616, ON_HOLD 9,804, SUSPECTED_FRAUD 4,062, CANCELED 3,692, PAYMENT_REVIEW 1,893. Rule: CANCELED and SUSPECTED_FRAUD are excluded from every revenue, profit and delivery KPI (their `Delivery Status` is `Shipping canceled`). Pending, processing, on-hold and payment-review items count as booked revenue; a stricter COMPLETE + CLOSED-only view would drop more than half the data.
- **Late flag.** `actual_days > scheduled_days` agrees with `Late_delivery_risk` except on cancelled shipments (rule T06), where the source sets risk to 0 even when the days say late. Those rows are excluded from KPIs anyway.
- **Shifted rows.** 3 customer rows have the city in the street column and a ZIP code in the state column. Staging moves them back (rule T11).
- **Market rotates with time.** In an average month 89% of items go to a single market (lowest month: 43%). Market is confounded with calendar period, so market-level trends are not comparable and market effects are tested within period where it matters.
- **Regime change from October 2017.** Up to September 2017 an order averages ~3 items; from October 2017 every order has exactly one item, the catalogue switches to different products, and 99% of buyers are first-time customers. Revenue then falls each month to January 2018. Trends, cohorts and the memo use the **analysis window Jan 2015 to Sep 2017 (33 full months)**; the full range stays in the warehouse (rule T12).
